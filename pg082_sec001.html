5. Data analysis

The data collected during an investigation must be analysed. This involves organising, sorting and arranging data properly. For example, you can compare the length of the amaranth plants from the watered and unwatered gardens to identify the differences in their growth.

6. Interpretation of results

In this step, the analysed data are interpreted in order to obtain meaningful information. This may enable others who are interested in the study to understand the problem and solutions. The interpreted results can be presented through descriptions, tables, figures, charts and graphs. An example is presented in the following table.
